# Card B: Individual contributor

**You're a manager now, reports or not.** Running an agent is delegation, and you can practise it
without ruining anyone's week.

## Per task

| # | Step (practice number) | Your move |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Outcome** (01) | Say what done looks like, plus three sentences: who the customer is, who uses it, what it's for. Ask for the plan, not the finished work. |
| 2 | **Pre-mortem, if it matters** (05) | "It's next month and this failed. What went wrong?" Then build the smallest useful piece. |
| 3 | **Checkpoint** (02) | Run it step by step with a yes/no check after each. Tell it which decisions it may make alone and which come back to you. |
| 4 | **Define good** (04) | Review closely, fix, and note every correction you make. |
| 5 | **Write it down** (04) | "Unpack what we just fixed into the skill, with a reason for each rule. Bump the version." Test it once in a fresh chat. |
| 6 | **Next time** (04) | Run the skill to do the work, and to check anyone else's: "80% there, change these." |

## Every week

Pick one bottleneck in your own work, the thing that eats the most time, and go all the way down on it.

## If this is you

- **No team setup?** A folder of skill docs in Drive or OneDrive is enough. Attach the one you need to the chat.
- **Boss not into AI?** Find exactly what they push back on, write it into your skills, and repeat until the output matches your best work.
- **Stepping away?** Tell the agent to stop only for critical decisions. Later, turn that into a written decision rule (see `checkpoint-designer`).
