# Completed staff work

Completed staff work means the decision arrives so finished that the reader only has to say yes, no
or "go with option two". The same discipline stops an agent from bouncing every small question back
to you. What comes up is the problem, the options and a recommendation, not a bare question.

## Steps

1. **Check it needs to come up at all.** Is it a one-way door (hard to reverse, costly, public, or
   outside what the person or agent was given authority to decide)? If it's cheap to reverse, decide
   it, log it, and move on. Say that in one line instead of writing the page.
2. **Write the problem** in at most three sentences: what is happening, why it needs a decision now,
   and what happens if the decision stalls. Use the reader's terms, not the team's shorthand.
3. **List two to four real options.** Include doing nothing if it's a live choice. Each option gets
   cost, effort, risk, and what it locks in. Say what would be hard to reverse.
4. **Recommend one.** Say which, and the one reason that decides it. A recommendation that says
   "it depends" isn't one. Name what would change your mind.
5. **State the ask** as a single reply the reader can send: "Approve option B?"
6. **Check, then fix.** Can the reader act with no other document? Is the recommendation one option?
   Are the facts sourced and any gaps marked rather than guessed? Is it one page? Fix and re-check.

## Why these rules

- Work done to a decision-ready state moves faster than a question the boss must research first.
- Real options keep the recommendation honest. A page with one option is a request for a rubber stamp.
- Stating what is hard to reverse is the part reviewers most often need and least often get.

## Template

Use `one-pager-template.md`. Keep the headings. Reviewers learn where to look.

## Example

Input: an agent running a content refresh finds that two blog posts rank for the same keyword.

Output:

> **Problem.** Two published posts compete for the same search term, and neither ranks well. A decision is needed this week because the refresh will edit one of them next.
> **Options.** A) Merge into the stronger post and redirect the other: one page to maintain, the old URL is permanently retired. B) Keep both, rewrite the weaker one for a different term: more work, nothing lost. C) Leave as is: no effort, the overlap continues.
> **Recommendation.** B. It keeps both URLs live and we can still merge later, whereas a redirect is hard to undo. Would change if the weaker post has no inbound links.
> **Ask.** Approve option B?
